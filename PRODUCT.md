# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three audiences, mostly on phones, task-focused and price-sensitive:
- **Duluth parents** registering kids for the summer **day camp** or the winter **ski & snowboard programs**, buying **season passes / rentals**, and checking hours, events, and dates. This is the primary conversion.
- **Local families & community** checking what's happening (Fall Fest, events), how to volunteer, and how to donate/support the hill.
- **Kids & teens** into the **terrain park / freestyle snowboarding** — the identity core; the page should feel like *theirs*.

Context: overwhelmingly mobile, quick in-and-out ("register my kid," "what are hours," "how much"), and cost-conscious — affordability + scholarships are central to why people trust this place.

## Product Purpose

The public web home for the **Chester Bowl Improvement Club** — a beloved Duluth non-profit **neighborhood kids' ski hill + summer day camp** in City-owned Chester Park (est. 1980). It exists to get families to register and participate, and to rally community support. Success = a fast, mobile-first, genuinely *alive* site that makes registering + finding info effortless and that captures Chester Bowl's punky, kid-joyful, community identity — the opposite of the current dated, thin, not-mobile-friendly WordPress site.

## Brand Personality

**Punky · kid-joyful · community-rooted.** Voice: fun, unpretentious, a little rebellious — freestyle-snowboard-and-terrain-park energy — but warm, inclusive, and proud. It's a DIY neighborhood hill run by volunteers, not a corporate resort. Emotional goals: excitement (kids sending it on the hill), belonging (this is *our* hill), trust (parents comfortable registering their kids), and a proud thread of nostalgia (the giant ski jumps that live on in the logo).

## Anti-references

- Generic AI/SaaS landing pages: gradient blobs, uniform icon-card grids, cream-plus-one-accent "dashboard" look, hero-metric templates, tracked-uppercase eyebrows on every section. (My own first attempt fell into this — explicitly rejected.)
- Corporate-nonprofit **beige / muted-earth-tone** default (their current site's exact failure mode).
- Stuffy/sepia **vintage-heritage / museum-plaque** treatment — honor the ski jumps as a proud sticker, not an antique.
- Over-cutesy/illegible, or edgelord-punk. It's for kids and parents: legible and joyful, with attitude.
- Anything that reads as a polished national ski resort. This is a little hill with a big heart.

## Design Principles

1. **Made by someone who loves this hill.** DIY craft and personality over templated polish — cut-and-paste sticker energy, hand-drawn touches, not a stock frame.
2. **The logo is the crest.** Celebrate the beloved black-and-white ski-jump emblem; keep it pure black; let the whole design orbit it. Honor the giant ski jumps' nostalgia (a proud beat, never sepia).
3. **Freestyle-first.** The terrain park / snowboarding is the loudest, most alive part of the page — the punk comes from there, not from decoration.
4. **Two clear seasons, one tap apart.** Summer (camp/events) and winter (the ski hill) are distinct purposes; auto-default to the current season, toggle to the other.
5. **Affordable, for every kid.** Lead with *register* and the scholarship promise — money never keeps a kid off the hill.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first is the top requirement (the current site fails here). WCAG AA contrast (body text ≥4.5:1) even with the punchy punk palette — never sacrifice legibility for attitude. Reduced-motion alternatives for all animation. Real, descriptive alt text on the real Chester Bowl photos. Copy readable and reassuring for parents completing registration.
